Phase 4 update for Aditya sir

How long does a vision-language model need to look at the image?

It depends less on what the task is about and more on one thing: whether the model has to write the answer or pick it from options.

P. S. Kedar, Research Intern, IIT Jodhpur
Supervisor: Prof. Divya Saxena  ·  October 2026

The test

Take the image away after layer L, and see what the model still gets right

  • Every visual token is deleted after layer L. The question keeps going up the network without the picture.
  • Layer 0 means no image at all. That gives the floor: what the model scores from the question alone.
  • Run for every L, on 8 tasks and 3 models: Qwen2.5-VL 7B, Qwen2.5-VL 3B and InternVL2 2B.

How we read one curve (Qwen 7B, MMBench)

With the image: 88. Without it: 40. So the image is worth 48 points.

The horizon is the layer after which the model holds on to at least half of those 48 points for good. Here that is layer 10 of 28.

The catch

Every early task was multiple choice. Every late task made the model write its answer.

So "reading needs the image longer" and "writing the answer needs the image longer" were tangled together. GQA was the first hint, because it mixes both kinds of question:

"What brand is on the bottle?"

Open-ended

The model has to spell out "Coca-Cola". It needs the actual letters, so it keeps looking until late.

What it means for pruning

A pruner can read the format from the prompt, and drop the image early only when that is safe

Share of layers that no longer carry visual tokens, with every task keeping at least 98% of its accuracy

one cut for every input cut chosen by answer format
  • Without the format, one cut has to protect the reading tasks, so it barely moves from the top.
  • With it, Qwen 7B keeps the image for all 28 layers on open questions and drops it after layer 20 on closed ones.
  • For comparison, the released BTP deletion saves 21% on Qwen 7B but leaves the worst task at 22% of its accuracy.
  • These numbers come straight from stored runs, which record right or wrong for each question at each layer. Cuts learned on the 8 tasks also held on the format-control sets they never saw.

Where this leaves us

What I think we can claim, and where I would like your view

The claim

How long a vision-language model needs its visual tokens depends mainly on the answer format. The ordering of tasks is stable across model sizes and families, and a pruner that knows the format saves 17 to 23% of visual layers at 98% accuracy, where a single cut saves under 8%.

What could weaken it

  • Three models, two families, 200 to 300 questions per task.
  • Reading questions turned into multiple choice still score 0.51 to 0.61 with no image, so the options alone rule some answers out.
  • Open-ended MMBench and AI2D score only 0.28 to 0.40, so those curves are noisier.
  • Content still matters a little: ChartQA keeps about a third of its gap even as multiple choice.

Questions for you

  1. For the paper, should the framing be "answer format" rather than "task families"?
  2. Next step: detect the format from the prompt inside the model, and add a larger model or a third family?
  3. Is this ready to take to ma'am, or should we close any of the gaps on the left first?
